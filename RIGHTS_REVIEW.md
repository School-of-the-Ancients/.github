# Rights review before a commercial agreement

This is a checklist of unresolved evidence, not a claim that the portfolio is
cleared for sale. Project-owned code and third-party inputs must be distinguished.

- Preserve dated Git history, original notices, release manifests, and private
  permission records. Do not edit old releases to imply earlier restrictions.
- Confirm the actual authors and applicable employment, university, collaboration,
  and contractor agreements. Git authorship alone is not conclusive.
- Review School's older combined distributions: it lacked a standalone license
  but was included alongside Matrix's root MIT notice. Do not assume either no
  grant or an exclusive grant without examining the exact distribution.
- Reconcile the bundled Professor VRMs' personalNonProfit metadata and upstream
  VRoid terms. Their commercial use and public character-generation use are
  different questions. Changing metadata is not a substitute for permission.
- Establish source and rights for legacy portraits, banners, audio, model weights,
  research PDFs, and other non-code files. Citing a paper does not sublicense it.
- Resolve the missing license text for the tr46 0.0.3 dependency in the current
  School package before representing its notice inventory as complete.
- Identify MIT/CC BY and other permissively distributed code copied between
  prototypes. New notices do not make those older portions exclusive.
- Review project names and logos before claiming registered marks or exclusive
  brand rights. Do not imply endorsement by OpenAI, Meta, pixiv, or historical figures.
- Have counsel review any assignment, patent provision, exclusivity promise,
  license warranty, or contributor agreement before signing. No broad warranty
  of sole ownership or freedom from infringement is made by these documents.

Keep private evidence and negotiation details outside public repositories.
