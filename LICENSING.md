# Licensing and intellectual-property policy

Reviewed October 27, 2026. This is repository guidance, not a copyright assignment,
legal opinion, commercial contract, or organization-wide software license.

## Use the terms for the material you actually received

Each repository, version, dependency, and asset may have different terms. A project
name, public GitHub page, or common maintainer does not put everything under one
license. Preserve explicit license grants and attribution in code, READMEs,
release bundles, and upstream notices.

A new notice cannot cancel an earlier valid MIT or Creative Commons grant.
Recipients may continue to use properly licensed earlier material under its
existing terms. They do not automatically gain rights to separately licensed new
contributions. Open-source licenses grant permissions; they do not transfer
copyright ownership. Lawful exceptions, public-domain material, and GitHub's own
platform permissions remain applicable.

## Direction for new development

The owner has selected PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0 for new project-owned material
in the current Matrix and Professor applications. Application repositories are
private; authorized recipients must read the LICENSE and notices at the exact
revision supplied. This does not change the terms of older releases or imply
that every file in a mixed package has the same license.

PolyForm is source-available, not OSI-approved open source. Read its complete
permitted-purpose provisions; some personal and institutional uses are allowed.
Uses outside those permissions require separate written terms from the relevant
rights holders. No commercial fee, paid support entitlement, exclusive deal, or
automatic permission to integrate into a commercial product is promised here.

Do not automatically publish new proprietary work under MIT, CC0, or another broad
grant. Choose and document the license before distribution. Keep unpublished
commercial work in a separate private development location where appropriate;
putting a file in a public branch or PR is still disclosure. Changing visibility
later does not retrieve copies already distributed.

## Older repositories and unclear terms

Older prototypes retain the grants they actually made. In particular, the
sota-alpha README declares MIT; the Horizon Worlds repository declares MIT for
code and CC BY 4.0 for models/art. Preserve their scope and attribution. Do not
replace those declarations with a notice claiming all rights were withdrawn.

Where no general license was identified, these administrative documents grant
no new copyright license. Public availability or the words "open source" alone
do not identify the scope of a particular grant. Check complete history,
embedded notices, bundles, upstream sources, and any separate permission.
"No license file found" is a review finding, not proof of exclusive ownership.

## Contributors and third-party material

Rights remain with their respective holders. A Git author line, an organization
membership, or an AI-generated artifact does not prove a complete chain of title.
Record contributions and their agreed terms. No contributor assigns copyright or
commercial relicensing rights merely by opening an issue or PR.

Before importing code, art, a model, audio, a dataset, or a document, record its
source, version/hash, license, attribution, restrictions, and permission evidence.
Preserve required notices. Do not label the entire asset library with the code
license. API terms, model-weight terms, and generated-output rights are separate.

The repository license is not permission to publish private learner records,
room imagery, conversations, credentials, or another person's work. It does not
establish trademark registration, exclusive rights in an idea, or the right to
imitate someone's voice or likeness.

## Commercial inquiries and review

Contact [the maintainer](https://github.com/thetopham) to discuss a particular
commercial use or partnership. Identify the repository, commit, assets, and
proposed use. A discussion or public issue is not a license or confidentiality
agreement; exchange sensitive material only through an agreed private channel.

Before an exclusive license, assignment, or commercialization claim, obtain a
focused review of the actual ownership, employment/university obligations,
earlier releases, dependencies, and asset permissions. Preserve relevant dated
source and permission records. Do not promise to revoke existing grants.
